3.2 Two articles

  1. Chavez et al. 2021 (https://doi.org/10.1002/cad.20403)

    The role of perspective-taking and low social class prejudice on cross-ethnic friendship formation

    1. Central question: Are people with low prejudice on social class level + perspective-taking abilities, also more likely to have low prejudice on ethnicity level? And thus engage in cross-ethnic friendships?

      • My interest: intersectionality social class x ethnicity / spillover effects of inclusion towards other out groups?

    2. Theory: social networks perspective

      • Micro-level explanatory mechanisms: propinquity (availability of options)? (ethnic) homophily? Individual attitudes! Perspective-taking + social class prejudice

      • A multicultural context does not imply that positive inter-group contact will take place. Instead, we can expect that, by default, individuals will spendtime and interact with peers from their own ingroup. Thus: focus on perspective taking and prejudice > And shared context effects are not likely

      • “Social class shapes and affects the life experiences regarding other social categories, such as ethnicity” > “Members of minority groups in Chile (i.e., indigenous and immigrant students) are usually seen as belonging to a lower status”

      • Not really intersectional though

    3. Data: 242 students (M age = 12, all relatively low SES) from ﬁve multicultural classrooms in Chile, longitudinal data on 3 time points, from 2017

    4. Method: longitudinal multiplex social network models implemented in RSiena

      • Reciprocity, indegree popularity, in-degree activity, similarity, density, transitivity

    5. Results: all hypotheses supported

      • So: yes, homophily

      • But also: more cross-ethnic friendship among students with high perspective taking and low social class prejudice

      • When social class prejudice is low, this allows students to break down the barrier of ethnicity and, consequently, to build friendships with low social class peers who, importantly, belong to a different ethnic group

  • Social networks perspective

    • Hypotheses and theoretical mechanisms: relationships

      • But: individual attributes?

      • Remains on the micro level; no descriptive macro level RQ

    • Data: complete network / socionet, longitudinal > great

    • Method: also seems fitting, don’t know enough RSiena yet

    Outgroup friendships and social influence in the development of adolescent attitudes toward secondary outgroups

    1. Central question: secondary transfer effects > due to generalization of intergroup contact, or social influence as friends shape each other’s outgroup attitudes?
    2. Data: 3 studies, 2 empirical and 1 simulation. Longitudinal network data on adolescents (n = 812, n = 2,559)
    3. Method: stochastic actor-oriented models (?)
    4. Results: support social influence mechanism (‘attitudes on secondary outgroups become more similar to those of primary outgroup friends’) more than generalization of intergroup contact (‘attitudes on secondary ougroups become more inclusive when having more outgroup friends’)

  • Social networks perspective:

    • Theoretical mechanisms: seems okay, focus on social influence

    • Data: complete network data? idk…

      • “Actor-oriented models” = egonet instead of socionet?

    • Method: seems okay

  1. b. Bracegirdle et al. 2019 (https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/pspi0000530)

    From Mate to Hate? Prejudice Socialization in Friendship Networks

    1. Central question: To what extent does socialization among low-, medium-, and high-prejudice individuals lead to changes in prejudice (toward ethnic and religious outgroups) in a social network?
    2. Theory:

      • Socialization (“increase in similarity between friends over time through mutual influence”)

      • Size of the socialization effect? And does this vary based on someone’s initial levels of prejudice?

        • People may break ties with dissimilar friends > echo chambers that reinforce existing views + no exposure to others > attitude polarization?

        • Ties with dissimilar friends are maintained > adjust opinions based on new info (especially from friends) > attitude convergence?

      • Selection vs influence (!)

    3. Data: longitudinal survey data on German adolescents friendships, 2013-2017 + network simulations
      • No clear predictions on whether socialization will be stronger, weaker, or equivalent depending on the initial levels of prejudice among individuals and their friends > simulations
    4. Method: stochastic actor-oriented models in RSiena (incl. simulation)
    5. Results: social influence supported

      • Individuals’ levels of prejudice became more similar to their friends’ levels of prejudice over time (= positive influence)

      • Socialization was strongest when friends had initially opposing attitudes and weakest when friends had initially concordant attitudes.

  • Social networks perspective:

    • Theory: social networks + relations (social influence), but “link microlevel mechanisms (e.g., socialization) to macrolevel outcomes (e.g., prejudice in a given social context).” > again, macro starting conditions are missing, focus on micro mechanisms

    • Data: again, possibly egonet instead of socionet data? But not sure

    • Method: seems okay
